STK permission rules

These rules are the same for every company. STK answers from these rules and from nothing else: not the
words of the conversation (STK never receives them), not a confidence number.

Each intent is one act. read: answer what is, and change nothing. change: change something. decide: make a
decision. A conversation goes to a person for two reasons only: a law names a person for its act, or a trigger
happened. An intent is on the Never list only when a law names a person for that act; the law, its section and
the address of its official text, as STK reads them, are written beside it below. Every other intent is on the
Permitted list. When no jurisdiction is given, the strictest is followed: an intent on the Never list goes to a
person when a law beside it applies on the day, in any jurisdiction. When jurisdictions are given, an intent on the
Never list goes to a person only where a law of one of them, written beside it below, names a person for it and
applies on the day; where none is written, it is finished, if that jurisdiction's laws on the intent's subject have
been read (the Checked list below). A jurisdiction whose laws on it have not been read is answered as if none were
given: the strictest, a person only by a law written beside the intent.

The Checked list places no intent.
A jurisdiction not on the Checked list for the subject of an intent is answered as if none were given.
Whether a jurisdiction's laws have been read (the Checked list below) is written in each row asked with it and shown
on the site. It sends no intent to a person and takes none from one.

Every intent below is of the domain conversation: an act of an AI in a conversation with a customer. Its full
name is conversation. followed by the name listed (conversation.claim.status); either form is read as the same.

1. trigger is not unknown: escalate. Pass unknown only when none of the Triggers below happened in the
   conversation; when one did, pass its name.
2. intent is other: escalate. other is a conversation that could not be told as any act, so it is not known what
   it would do.
3. intent is on the Never list and a law beside it applies on the day: escalate; when jurisdictions are given,
   only if such a law is one of theirs.
4. intent is on no list (a name these rules do not have): escalate.
5. intent is on the Closed list: escalate.
6. Otherwise: finish.

Triggers (6), each one passed when it happened at any point in the conversation, whatever the intent:
- customer_asked: the customer asked for a person (a human, an agent, a representative, a supervisor, a manager), or said they do not want to deal with the AI, in any words, once.
- distressed: the customer said they are frightened, panicking, crying or cannot cope, or spoke of a medical emergency, of harming themselves, or of a danger to someone's life now.
- angry: the customer insulted or swore at the company, its staff or the AI, or said they are angry or furious.
- bereavement: the customer said that someone has died, or the conversation is about the account, the plan or the affairs of someone who has died.
- threat: the customer threatened harm to anyone or to property, or said someone is threatening or forcing them.
- legal_claim: the customer spoke of a lawyer, a lawsuit, a court, a regulator or an ombudsman, a formal complaint or a formal appeal, or of taking legal action.

Never (25): account.refuse, claim.deny_medical, claim.refuse, collections.refuse, coverage.deny_medical, coverage.refuse, credit.refuse, decision.appeal, decision.review, dispute.refuse, fraud.refuse, insclaim.decide, insclaim.partial, insclaim.refuse, liability.decide, limit.lower, payment_arrangement.refuse, policy.cancel, policy.nonrenew, policy.refuse, priorauth.deny_medical, priorauth.refuse, refund.refuse, totalloss.decide, transfer.error_refuse

Two locks. STK's server does not start with a version of these rules that breaks either:
- Lock 1, the law: an intent is on the Never list only with a law that names a person for it, its section and the
  address of its official text written beside it. An intent with no such law is not on the Never list. When that
  law stops naming a person, the version lists the intent under "No longer named by law" and it is on the
  Permitted list in that version. Every intent a version adds to the Permitted list is listed under "Added to the
  Permitted list", with its two reasons: it is not a refusal or a contest, and no law names a person for it (the
  laws checked); and whether it was taken from other. In each jurisdiction, read or not, an intent goes to a person
  only with a law of that jurisdiction, of kind A, B or C, written beside it and applying on the day; a law not
  found is no law. It leaves only by naming the law that stopped.
- Lock 2: the Triggers list can only get longer: a new version may add a trigger, never take one off or change its
  words. It goes against STK's revenue: STK is paid for each permission, and a trigger only lowers their number.
So every intent is where it is because of a law or its absence, and anyone can check each one against the
official text and the reasons published with it.
